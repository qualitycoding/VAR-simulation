A verified adaptive-mesh solver for the 2D axisymmetric VAR model

A rebuild of a Vacuum Arc Remelting solver on body-fitted, solution-adaptive meshes, with the thermal problem verified to second order and the flow problem reported as not yet converged.

Technical note  GHSC Research
Model  R. Polton, Numerical Grid Generation And Its Application In The Solution Of A Model Of The Vacuum-Arc Remelting (VAR) Process, University of Southampton, 1998, chapter 4
Status  thermal problem verified; flow integrals outstanding
Software  Python, NumPy and SciPy only

1Summary

This note reports a solver, a verification, and an unfinished convergence study. The last of those is the reason the note exists in this form rather than as a results paper.

The equations are those derived in chapter 4 of the thesis above: coupled stream function, vorticity, energy and magnetic induction equations for an axisymmetric ingot, non-dimensionalised as (4.39) to (4.42), transformed onto a body-fitted mesh, with a D'Arcy term representing the liquid-solid transition. The parameters are its Table 4.1.

What is established here: the discrete operators are consistent and second-order accurate on curvilinear meshes; the energy equation converges to machine precision and its global heat balance converges at the order of the scheme; the magnetic induction equation is solved exactly with the thesis arc condition applied; and steady solutions exist along a continuation path spanning Grashof numbers from 2.9×106 to 2.0×108.

What is not established: the flow field is not yet independent of the mesh or of the regularisation applied to the phase transition. Kinetic energy and circulation still move by around a third across plausible regularisations at the finest resolution reached. The dependence is shrinking under refinement at roughly first order, which indicates a limit exists and is being approached, but the resolutions needed to demonstrate it were beyond the compute available for this note. Those quantities are reported below with that caveat attached and should not be quoted as converged.

scope of claims

What a reader may and may not take from this note

Only the global energy balance is supported by a mesh-convergence study with an observed order (2 to 3). The thermal field and melt-pool extent are stable to within about 2% across the meshes tried — molten volume 1.8%, thermal energy 0.11% — but no asymptotic order or error estimate has been demonstrated for them. The flow amplitude is not converged. The qualitative flow structure — a single Marangoni-driven cell beneath the pool — is robust across every parameter and mesh tried, but its magnitude is resolution-dependent at the resolutions shown.

2Formulation

The four governing equations are solved in the form given by the thesis, with the diffusion operators evaluated in conservative curvilinear form:

Cartesian part, conservative form lap(u) = (1/J) [ d_xi( (alpha u_xi - beta u_eta)/J ) + d_eta( (gamma u_eta - beta u_xi)/J ) ]

with the axisymmetric terms added through the metric expressions for uR and uZ. This is equivalent to the transformed equations (4.55) and (4.56) of the thesis but needs no explicit assembly of their first-derivative coefficients Δ1 and Γ1, which involve second derivatives of the coordinate fields and are awkward to difference stably.

Each equation is assembled as a sparse linear system and solved directly, so the residual of each discrete equation is zero to round-off after its own solve, and the only iteration is over the physical coupling. The stream function and vorticity are solved as one block: the vorticity sources the stream function through (4.39), and the stream function fixes the wall vorticity through (4.45) and (4.46), and that coupling is stiff enough that solving the two in sequence limit-cycles above Gr of about 5×106. Only the convective velocities are lagged.

Convergence is measured as the relative max-norm residual of each assembled system, never as the change between successive iterates. This distinction matters: an increment-based test applied to a heavily under-relaxed iteration reports convergence long before the solution arrives.

Deviations from the thesis, and why

D'Arcy sign. Equation (4.41) prints the D'Arcy group as +Cω on the left. Section 4.1 is explicit that the term exists to suppress motion below the melting temperature, that is, as a drag. With the drag sign the discrete operator is an M-matrix and the vorticity is driven towards zero in the solid; with the printed sign the diagonal changes sign once C exceeds the diffusive scale. The drag sign is used.

D'Arcy magnitude. C0 = 109 rather than the thesis 1010. At 1010 the residual stalls on the melting isotherm, where C jumps by about 109 between neighbouring nodes — which is the failure the thesis itself anticipates, quoting Hibbert, Markatos and Voller on C being so large as to cause rapid change in velocity and possible convergence failure. Where both converge, the two values give identical solutions to four significant figures.

Mushy-zone regularisation. The thesis uses a Heaviside step at the melting isotherm. Applied node-wise that step makes the fixed-point map discontinuous, since a node flipping between C = 0 and C = 109 between outer iterations moves the whole vorticity field. The step is instead ramped over a band a fixed number of computational cells wide, using the change in temperature across one cell as the ramp interval. Section 4 explains why this particular regularisation and not a simpler one.

The heat imbalance falls at observed pairwise orders 2.06, 2.48 and 3.06, consistent with the second-order accuracy established for the operators and, at the finest pair, somewhat better. This is the one thermal convergence claim in this note that carries an observed order.

The other integrals do not support an order estimate. Across the whole range from N = 51 to N = 101, molten volume changes by 1.8%, thermal energy by 0.11% and the surface temperature integral by 0.09%. That is small, but the successive changes do not decay as the mesh refines. Molten volume changes by −1.35×10-3 from N = 51 to 71, −3.9×10-4 from 71 to 81 and −8.1×10-4 from 81 to 101, which per unit of mesh spacing is slopes of −0.24, −0.22 and −0.32, not shrinking. Solving for an order from the three finest meshes gives no consistent value for molten volume or thermal energy, and 0.52 for the surface temperature integral. An earlier version of this note reported extrapolated errors of 0.99%, 0.073% and 0.029% for these three quantities. Those came from a fit that included an unconverged N = 76 point and are withdrawn. No error estimate is claimed for any of them.

Why N = 76 is not in the table. The iteration does not converge on that mesh. It settles onto a limit cycle of period 450 sweeps in which the residuals repeat exactly (the vorticity residual is 1.3×10-1 at sweeps 150, 600, 1050 and 1500), so this is not slow convergence and more iterations would not have helped. An earlier version of this note described the stall as running out of allotted time, which was wrong. Over one full cycle the heat imbalance varies by 3.2%, molten volume by 0.09%, thermal energy by 0.006%, kinetic energy by 1.1% and circulation by 0.9%. Taking N = 76 anywhere within its cycle would give orders of 2.08 to 2.16 and 2.89 to 3.00 either side of it, consistent with the converged sequence above. Under-relaxing the flow, under-relaxing the temperature, heavier pseudo-transient damping and Anderson acceleration all failed to remove it, and Anderson acceleration made the iteration erratic. The cycle is specific to this mesh: N = 71 and N = 81 converge to 10-11 and 10-13. N = 91 did not converge within its time budget and was not investigated further. One damping variant reached a residual of 10-3 before departing, which is consistent with a fixed point that exists but is unstable under this Picard iteration. That explanation has not been tested.

A note on what is not in that table. Point extrema such as the maximum stream function, and the pool depth located by interpolating the melting isotherm, were used as convergence metrics in an earlier version of this work and produced fitted orders of 0.16 and −6 — that is, nonsense. Neither is better than first order and both are sensitive to where nodes happen to fall. They are reported elsewhere in this note for description but are not used to assess convergence.

4The mushy-zone regularisation, and why the flow is not converged

This section is the reason the note is framed as it is. It is reported at length because the failure mode is instructive and is easy to miss.

Regularising the Heaviside requires choosing a band over which C varies. The obvious choices — a fixed width in temperature, or a fixed physical distance across the isotherm — both leave a free parameter. The kinetic energy varies by 76% and the circulation by 63%, monotonically, and the increments per unit width grow as the width shrinks. There is no limit being approached. The regularisation is not smoothing a discontinuity; it is setting the flow amplitude. Thermal quantities are almost untouched — 0.16% on the heat balance, 0.37% on thermal energy — which is consistent, since the energy equation does not see the D'Arcy term directly.

Replacing the fixed width with a band a fixed number of cells wide removes the free parameter in the continuum limit: the band narrows with the mesh and recovers the Heaviside as h tends to zero, while remaining a continuous function of the nodal temperatures. The relevant test is then whether the answer becomes band-independent under refinement. Spread between a one-cell and a three-cell band:

This is the behaviour a convergent regularisation should show, and the fixed-width formulation never showed it. But the flow spreads are still large. They fall at roughly first order — ratios of 1.41 then 0.94 across the two refinements — so bringing the kinetic-energy spread below 5% requires the mesh spacing to fall by about a further factor of six, or N of order 600. Two further signs that the asymptotic range has not been reached: at fixed band width the kinetic energy still climbs monotonically with N, and convergence failures reappear at the narrow end, where the stiffness that motivated the regularisation returns.

The molten volume, at 2.56%, is close to acceptable. The flow integrals are not. Extending the study to N of order 300 to 600 is a single long computation rather than anything algorithmically new, and the accompanying software includes a resumable script for it; it was not run for this note.

The electromagnetic body force is negligible here

With the thesis arc condition (4.32) applied, the magnetic induction equation solves exactly (residual 2.2×10-16) and gives a peak non-dimensional field of 0.3989. At the converged state the maximum buoyancy source in the molten region is 1.53×108 against a maximum Lorentz source of 1.83×104, a ratio of 1.2×10-4.

That ratio is invariant under the viscosity scaling used for the continuation, so it holds along the whole path. With the material parameters of Table 4.1 the Lorentz force does not drive this flow; the circulation is Marangoni-driven with a buoyancy contribution. This contradicts a claim made in an earlier draft of this work, which described a magnetic pinch opposing the surface flow. No such effect is present.

6Limitations

The flow amplitude is not converged. Stated in full in section 4. Kinetic energy, circulation and maximum stream function should be treated as indicative only.

The D'Arcy term does not arrest the solid. Velocity in the solid region is suppressed by about a factor of 12 relative to the liquid, not the three orders of magnitude specified by Hibbert, Markatos and Voller. This appears structural rather than a matter of tuning: the term acts on the vorticity equation, so it drives the solid irrotational, after which the stream function satisfies a homogeneous Poisson equation there and decays away from the pool without vanishing. The thesis anticipates the qualitative point in section 4.1, observing that non-zero velocity in the solid is unrealistic but acceptable given the advantage of a single equation across both regions. The quantitative size of the gap between the streamfunction-vorticity and primitive-variable formulations is reported here but has not been analysed, and the structural explanation above, while consistent with the evidence, has not been proved.

Continuation stops at Gr = 2.0×108. Beyond that the Picard iteration with lagged convection loses stability and no steady solution was obtained. Whether this is loss of a stable steady laminar branch or a limitation of the iteration has not been determined. A Newton method on the fully coupled system, or time-accurate integration, would settle it. No claim is made either way, and in particular the full unscaled Table 4.1 parameters (Gr = 2.9×1010) remain out of reach here.

The iteration limit-cycles on some meshes. At N = 76 (two-cell band, verification case) the Picard iteration settles onto a limit cycle rather than converging, as described in section 3. The cause is undiagnosed. The heat-balance order does not depend on it, since it is established from four meshes that converged. But the failure means the solver cannot yet be relied on to converge at an arbitrary resolution, and it may be what stopped N = 91 as well. A Newton method, or a smoother liquid-fraction function than the clipped-linear ramp used here, is the natural next test.

Thermal integrals other than the heat balance have no demonstrated order. Molten volume and thermal energy vary by 1.8% and 0.11% across N = 51 to 101, but their successive changes do not decay, so no error estimate is claimed.

Mesh-convergence evidence is at the verification case only. The refinement study was run at the section 4.5.1 parameters, where every case converges to machine precision quickly. At Gr = 2.0×108 convergence on coarse uniform meshes is slow enough that a comparable study was not possible within the compute available, so the demanding case is reported at the residual achieved rather than with a demonstrated order.

The adaptive mesh is a convenience, not a demonstrated accuracy gain. The adaptation clusters correctly and does not fold, and the adapted solution agrees with the uniform one. No claim is made that adaptation improved accuracy for a given node count; establishing that would need the converged flow field the note does not yet have.

7Software

Pure Python on NumPy and SciPy. Two components are worth noting for anyone reimplementing.

The grid generator solves the Thompson-Thames-Mastin elliptic system with control functions derived from a monitor function blending normalised vorticity magnitude and normalised temperature-gradient magnitude. It is not the Brackbill-Saltzman method, despite an earlier version of this software being named for it: Brackbill-Saltzman minimises a weighted sum of smoothness, orthogonality and volume integrals, whose Euler-Lagrange equations (thesis 3.14 and 3.21) contain none of the terms solved here. The forcing is scaled by the absolute Jacobian rather than its square, an empirical rescaling that compensates for normalising the computational spacings to unity, and which means the equation solved is not the stationarity condition of any stated functional.

Point relaxation on the grid equation does not converge usefully at practical resolutions. At 101×101 the residual falls like the reciprocal of the sweep count: 12,800 point Gauss-Seidel sweeps took it only from 2.7×10-1 to 6.4×10-2. Picard iteration with the metric coefficients frozen and a direct sparse solve reaches 2.1×10-7 in 27 outer iterations and about two seconds. Any implementation that runs a fixed, modest number of point sweeps is returning a partially relaxed mesh rather than a solution of its grid equation.

8Two errors made during this work

Both were caught by cross-checking rather than by the residual, and both are easy to repeat.

A fix that makes the residual behave is not thereby correct. Widening the mushy band to cure a residual stall produced a cleanly converged solution on a spurious branch, with the circulation reduced to a third and the pool depth cut by two thirds. It converged beautifully. It was wrong, and only a comparison against a stiffer, harder-to-converge run revealed it.

Convergence metrics have to be chosen before they are trusted. The first refinement study of this work used point extrema and an interpolated isotherm position and returned fitted orders of 0.16 and −6. The discretisation was not at fault; the functionals were, compounded by a regularisation whose physical width varied with the mesh. Reporting those orders as evidence of anything would have been an error of exactly the kind this rebuild was undertaken to correct.

9Outstanding work

  • Extend the band-independence study to N of order 300 to 600 and report converged flow integrals, or report the limit as unreachable by this method.
  • Determine whether the Gr = 2.0×108 ceiling is a bifurcation or an iteration failure.
  • Quantify, rather than assert, the irrotational leakage of the D'Arcy formulation, ideally against a primitive-variable solution of the same problem.
  • Find out why the iteration limit-cycles at N = 76, and whether N = 91 fails for the same reason. Test the unstable-fixed-point hypothesis with a Newton method or a C1-smooth liquid fraction.
  • Establish an asymptotic order for molten volume and thermal energy, which the converged meshes tried do not show.
  • Repeat the mesh-convergence study at the demanding parameters once the above is settled.
